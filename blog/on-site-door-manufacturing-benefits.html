Why On-Site Door Manufacturing Saves Builders Time and Money

For Builders · 2026-05-18 · 6 min read

Most door vendors manufacture in a factory and truck finished doors to site. It works — until it doesn't: transit damage, dispatch delays, mismatched batches, and a fitting contractor who blames the supplier for every defect. There is another model, and it's the one we've built our reputation on.

How the on-site model works

  1. Setup (weeks 1–3): We install machinery inside your project — hydraulic press, laminating, sliding saw, multi-boring — in a covered area you allocate.
  2. Production synced to slab cycle: Frames and shutters are manufactured floor by floor as towers become fitting-ready. No warehouse, no dispatch queue.
  3. Install-as-you-build: The same team manufactures, installs, fits hardware and polishes. One contract, one accountability.
  4. Exit: Machinery demobilises after the final tower's snag list closes.

What it eliminates

  • Transport damage: the single biggest source of door rework simply disappears — doors travel by lift, not truck.
  • Schedule slippage: production capacity sits inside your gate; a slab delay pauses production instead of creating a warehouse pile-up or a dispatch scramble.
  • Batch mismatch: one machine setup and one material lot per project means door #4,500 matches door #1.
  • The supplier/fitter blame game: when manufacturing and installation are one team, every defect has one owner.

The numbers behind the claim

At Roshan Milestone (Tathawade): 3150+ doors and 1800+ frames across 8 towers to the 14th floor, 100% completed in ~1.5 years, with an on-site laminating machine installed specifically to keep finishing consistent. At Yashada Splendid Park I & II (Moshi-Alandi): 4500+ doors, 2650+ frames and 90+ duct shafts across 9 towers in 2 years with a 20–25 person crew.

When on-site doesn't make sense

Honesty matters: below roughly 800–1,000 doors, the machinery mobilisation cost outweighs the savings. Those orders we manufacture at our 12,000 sq ft factory at Nere (near Punawale) and deliver with careful logistics — the on-site model earns its keep on township-scale work.

Frequently asked questions

What is on-site door manufacturing?

A model where the door contractor installs woodworking machinery inside the construction project and manufactures frames and shutters at the site itself, floor by floor, instead of trucking finished doors from a factory.

What are the benefits of on-site door manufacturing for builders?

Near-zero transport damage, production synced with the construction schedule, consistent finishing across thousands of doors, and single-team accountability for both manufacturing and installation.

At what project size does on-site manufacturing make sense?

Generally above 800–1,000 doors. Below that, machinery mobilisation costs outweigh the benefits and factory supply is more economical.
