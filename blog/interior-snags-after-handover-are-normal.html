Why Small Adjustments After Handover Are Normal — and What Actually Defines Quality

Straight Talk · 2026-07-10 · 4 min read

Sometimes clients feel that once a project is complete, no issue should ever come up again. And honestly — if you're getting interiors done for the first time, thinking this way is completely natural. You've paid good money, you've waited months, and you want it perfect. You should.

But here is something very few people explain before handover: an interior is not a ready-made product. It doesn't come sealed in a box from a factory shelf. It is built inside your home, to your home's exact walls and floors — by hand.

What actually happens on an interior project

A single project brings together many different materials — plywood, veneer, laminate, hardware, glass, stone, polish. It passes through many different craftsmen — carpenters, polishers, painters, fitters. And it happens in multiple stages, over weeks or months, in a live site where civil work, electrical work and weather are all playing their part.

When this much handwork comes together, a few small adjustments after completion are absolutely normal:

  • A shutter that needs a minor alignment
  • A drawer channel that needs re-setting
  • A silicone joint that needs a touch-up
  • A hinge that needs a small turn of the screwdriver

In the industry, we call these snags. Every project has them — the ₹5 lakh flat and the ₹5 crore bungalow alike. Wood is a natural material; it breathes, it settles, it responds to Pune's seasons. A company that promises you zero snags is either inexperienced or not being honest with you.

Snags don't decide quality. This does.

Here is the truth we've learned over 35+ years in this craft: the quality of a company is not decided by whether snags appear. It is decided by what the company does when they appear.

Does the company pick up your phone after the final payment is cleared? Does it come back to your site? Does it treat the snag as its own responsibility — or does it start explaining why it's not their problem?

Anyone can look professional while the payments are flowing. A company's real character shows in the weeks after handover, when there is nothing left to invoice — only a promise left to keep.

Our promise at Motion Woods

At Motion Woods, this thinking is fixed: if our client faces any issue after handover — any issue, of any kind — we resolve it in the best possible way we can. We pick up the phone. We come back to the site. We treat your snag list as unfinished work, not as a favour.

That is how the founder's team earned repeat contracts from Pune's most demanding developers across 7,650+ doors and dozens of interiors — not by never having a snag, but by never walking away from one.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal to have small issues after interior work is completed?

Yes — completely normal. Interiors are built by hand, on-site, from many materials across multiple stages. Minor adjustments after handover, like shutter alignment or drawer settings, appear on every project regardless of budget. These are called snags in the industry.

What is a snag in an interior project?

A snag is a minor post-completion adjustment — a shutter needing alignment, a drawer channel needing re-setting, a silicone joint needing touch-up, or a hinge needing tightening. Snags are routine and fixable; they do not indicate poor quality workmanship.

Does Motion Woods provide support after handover?

Yes. If a client faces any issue after handover, Motion Woods resolves it in the best possible way — we take the call, visit the site, and treat post-handover snags as our responsibility, not the client's problem.
